Brainstorming

A few ideas were explored before we settled on date seed as our base ingredient.

Considered

Eggshell powder

Agricultural waste rich in bioavailable calcium and trace minerals — supports bone, skin and joint health. Already used in nutraceuticals, cosmetics and soil enrichment.

Considered

Medical blister packs

Problem: a strip's brand/medicine name is printed once across the full strip, so a cut piece becomes unreadable.

Dropped — a single design fix, not a brand opportunity
Our base ingredient

Date seed

Date pits make up 10–15% of the fruit. With millions of tonnes of dates processed globally, this is a large, underutilised, near-zero-cost resource, mostly discarded or used as low-value feed.

03

Date seed in chocolate

My direction was to bring dates and date seeds into chocolate. The research I found showed date seed powder being used in chocolate formulations only recently (2025), which made the idea feel genuinely new.

Study 1

Valorization of Date Seed Powder for Development of Enriched Chocolate – A Sensory Evaluation Study

Loyola Academy, Secunderabad (2025). Chocolate was made with date seed powder at different concentrations. Sample B — 100 g cocoa powder, 13 g date seed powder, 40 g date syrup and 35 g butter — achieved the highest sensory score. Panelists noted balanced appearance, colour, aroma, taste and texture. Date seed powder improved colour, added a mild nutty flavour and enhanced nutrition without affecting stability.

8.5/9 sensory score for Sample B, at 13% date seed powder.

Palety, K. K., Madamolla, B. S., Koriginja, M., & Kambham, M. N. (2025). International Journal of Engineering Research & Technology (IJERT), 14(12). Read the study

Study 2

Utilization of the date seed powder in food manufacturing

Found that crude fibre and total ash content increased as roasted date seed powder levels rose. Concluded that roasted date seed powder is suitable for chocolate production and may offer anti-cancer properties.

Bashir, M. M. A. (2026). Sustainable Food Technology, 4(2), 2187–2197. Royal Society of Chemistry. Read the study

Where the research ends and my direction begins

Study 1 made its chocolate from cocoa powder, date syrup and butter, and does not label it dark or milk. So the 13% result is a starting point I am carrying into dark chocolate, not a finding that has been shown for dark chocolate. Everything below is my own reasoning built on these studies, and would need testing in a real formulation.

Date seeds are almost universally discarded, so the ingredient story can be about waste valorisation rather than sourcing something premium.

13% date seed powder is where I would begin the formulation, since it scored highest in the study, then test it in a dark base.

I chose dark chocolate because I expect its natural bitterness and depth to suit the nutty, coffee-like quality of date seed powder.

Natural spice and fruit flavours (cardamom, orange, ginger, cinnamon, mango, rose) could be layered into the base formulation for variety.

In my search I did not find a dark chocolate with date seed powder in the Indian market, which suggests genuinely new territory.

05

5W1H

Framing the product, its buyer, and its context in one pass.

What What is the product? A premium Indian dark chocolate made with upcycled date seed powder, naturally sweetened with date syrup, in six distinct Indian flavours — sold as bars or curated gift sets. What problem does it solve? Bridges generic and luxury gifting, offering indulgent chocolate that's healthier, functional and guilt-free.
Why Why would someone buy this? Removes the usual trade-offs — a premium, Indian, healthy, unique gift that feels meaningful to give, unlike generic or overpriced options. Why date seeds? A widely discarded yet nutrient-rich ingredient — making the waste-to-value story authentic and ownable.
Who Who is buying it? Urban Indians (22–35) in Tier 1 cities — personal gifters and corporate buyers seeking thoughtful, story-driven, health-conscious gifts. Who is receiving it? A broad audience (16–55) — the product must feel personal to both giver and receiver.
When When do people buy this? Diwali, New Year, birthdays and corporate gifting — October–November is the peak buying window. When do people eat it? As a mindful treat, post-meal or during breaks, savoured slowly.
Where Where is it sold? Primarily D2C (website, Instagram), plus quick commerce, premium retail, specialty food outlets, cafés and corporate bulk channels. Where is it consumed? At home or work, during slow, intentional moments — not eaten on the go.
How How does the buyer discover it? Primarily Instagram (Reels, shares), word-of-mouth, lifestyle features (The Hindu, Condé Nast Traveller India, Vogue India), and direct corporate outreach before Diwali.

Market TG (wider audience): since this is a gifting product, the realistic buyer-and-receiver universe spans 16–55 years — someone's aunt could receive this at Diwali, a 19-year-old could gift it to a friend on their birthday. This wider market is referenced for commercial scope, but all design decisions serve the 22–30 primary audience.
